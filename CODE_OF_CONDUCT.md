# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the work
rather than about each other.

## What is expected

- **Say what you mean, plainly.** Directness is welcome; contempt is not.
- **Criticise the code, not the person who wrote it.** "This will break on
  Windows because paths are joined with a slash" is useful. "This is amateur
  work" is not, and is usually less specific as well.
- **Assume good faith.** Most disagreements here are two people holding
  different facts, not one person being difficult.
- **Accept a decision once it is made**, including one that goes against you.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination on any grounds, sexualised
language or imagery, publishing anyone's private information, and sustained
disruption of a discussion.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and code
review — anywhere connected to this project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately through
[GitHub's report form](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse), or by opening a
private security advisory if that is the only private channel available to you.

Reports are read by the maintainer. What happens next is proportionate: usually
a word, sometimes an edit, occasionally a block. Nobody is owed a public
explanation of a private report, and the person who reported it will not be
named without their agreement.

## Attribution
